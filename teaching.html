Teaching

I want students to understand not only the principles of ecology and evolution but also how we study them. Coding is part of my research every day, so I bring it into the classroom through interactive Jupyter notebooks written for students with no programming experience. Each one runs in the browser, so students don’t need to install anything.

Mentoring

From 2023 to 2026 I directed field, lab, and computational projects on pollen–pistil interactions at the Rocky Mountain Biological Laboratory and Columbia, supervising two undergraduates, a master’s student, and two community scientists. Undergraduate and master’s mentees are coauthors on two of the resulting papers. I also mentored students through the Barnard Summer Research Institute and Science Pathways Scholars Program, the RMBL Buddies Program, Científico Latino, and Columbia Women in Science, and chaired Undergraduate Outreach in Ecology and Evolution, a graduate student group that runs professional development workshops for Barnard and Columbia undergraduates.

Before graduate school, I spent a year as a program assistant for the School for Field Studies in Peru’s lowland Amazon, leading field exercises and tutoring undergraduates on their independent projects, especially statistics.
